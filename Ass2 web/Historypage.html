AROMA CAFE

History

Aroma is the primary sensory driver of coffee, created entirely during the roasting process when heat transforms a mostly odorless green coffee bean through complex chemical reactions. Raw green beans naturally lock away nonvolatile sugars, lipids, amino acids, and chlorogenic acids. As roasting temperatures climb, thermal decomposition (pyrolysis) and the fundamental Maillard reaction take over, breaking down these locked elements to generate up to 1,000 distinct volatile organic compounds. These rising gasses and vapors—such as aldehydes, pyrazines, and furans—burst through the bean's cell walls to release the distinct fruity, nutty, or caramelized scents that human olfactory receptors perceive as the classic "fresh-brewed" coffee fragrance
